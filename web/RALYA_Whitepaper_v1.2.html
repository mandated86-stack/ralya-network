1. Executive summary

RALYA is being built as economic settlement infrastructure for work performed between autonomous software, AI agents, machines and human service providers. The long-term objective is to let one participant commission work from another, define practical payment terms, place economic value at risk and settle outcomes under transparent rules.

RLYA is the fixed-supply protocol asset intended to provide bonding, collateral, staking and economic accountability. It is not intended to be the mandatory payment currency for every job. Practical assets such as USDC can remain the payment rail for work itself while RLYA supplies economic security underneath that activity.

Important scope: the token and settlement foundation are being released before the broader Jobs / AI-agent work modules. RALYA does not claim that arbitrary AI-work verification is already solved.

2. Autonomous-work settlement model

  1. Request: an AI agent, software system, machine, company or person defines a task.
  2. Settlement: practical payment terms can be specified in an asset such as USDC.
  3. Bond: the provider commits RLYA or another approved security position.
  4. Work and evidence: the result and task-specific evidence are submitted.
  5. Verification / dispute: deterministic tasks can be tested automatically; subjective tasks require an appropriate resolution mechanism.
  6. Settlement and reputation: accepted work releases payment and returns the bond; failure can expose the bond to protocol-defined consequences.

3. Why RLYA exists

RLYA is designed to be the security asset underneath the protocol rather than the compulsory currency for every task. Planned uses include provider bonds, staking, collateral around economic promises, dispute/security participation and future protocol-security roles.

This allows a buyer to price a service in a practical asset such as USDC while a provider's RLYA position supplies economic accountability.

4. Solana-first architecture

RALYA begins on Solana rather than operating an independent validator network before real usage exists. The release architecture includes a dedicated sale/founder-lock program designed to verify the fixed supply, control the official presale inventory, maintain the stepped price curve, support the later atomic USDC-to-RLYA sale path, enforce referral accounting, reconcile pre-launch allocations and enforce the founder lock.

7. Two-stage presale architecture

Stage A - Pre-launch allocation. A buyer connects a Solana wallet, authenticates a short-lived quote, pays real Solana USDC, and the backend independently verifies the confirmed transaction before recording the exact RLYA allocation against that wallet. Reconnecting the same wallet displays the confirmed expected RLYA. Distribution is scheduled before public token launch.

Stage B - Post-launch atomic settlement. After production RLYA distribution and the deliberate public token launch, the existing sale path supports USDC-to-RLYA settlement in the same Solana transaction, with program pricing and minimum-output protection.

A pre-launch allocation is shown as an allocation, not falsely presented as an already-delivered token balance.

9. One curve for website and private/off-site allocations

Authorized private/off-site investor allocations consume the same 100.68M presale pool and advance the same fixed curve. The owner does not get an arbitrary replacement public-price field. The exact RLYA amount is recorded, and that amount advances total confirmed allocation.

10. Referral distribution

The fixed referral rate is 1% of gross referred USDC. The buyer pays the normal gross amount and receives the normal RLYA allocation; 1% goes to the locked referrer and 99% to the configured presale treasury. No additional RLYA is created. First confirmed referral attribution is permanent for that buyer wallet; self-referrals and direct two-wallet circular loops are rejected.

11. Pre-launch records and distribution

The pre-launch ledger separates verified website USDC allocations from authorized private/off-site allocations. After allocation access is closed and in-flight quotes have cleared, the owner exports a hashed final delivery manifest.

Production distribution uses the official sale vault. Website allocations import their verified gross USDC/referral accounting and advance the shared total-sold curve without being mislabeled as manual sales. Private/off-site allocations advance the separate manual counter. Deterministic per-wallet receipt accounts prevent duplicate distribution on reruns.

12. Public reveal timing is separate

Technical completion does not force a public launch date. The public website can move through Pre-launch, Mainnet preparation, Mainnet verified, Distribution preparation, Distribution scheduled and Launch approaching. These messaging stages cannot mint tokens, move funds, resume the sale or open trading.

13. Founder lock

The founder allocation is exactly 83,900,000 RLYA (10%) and is part of the fixed supply. The production program is designed to lock that allocation for 365 days beginning with activation while the final committed sale state can remain PAUSED.

14. Narrow token rules

The launch architecture does not include post-launch RLYA minting, transfer taxes, hidden token blacklists or an arbitrary owner-editable public price. Owner-authorized distributions operate only against the fixed presale inventory and fixed pricing model.

15. Verification research for autonomous work

The hardest RALYA problem is not payment; it is proving that arbitrary work was completed correctly without making one company the universal judge. RALYA treats verification as a family of mechanisms: deterministic tests, machine-verifiable evidence, competitive reproduction/challenge, buyer acceptance windows, dispute mechanisms and economic-history reputation. The token launch does not claim this research problem is solved.

17. Open-source evidence

The public repository is intended to expose the sale program, economic model, tests, website clients, owner tools, threat model, whitepaper source and launch evidence. Open source is not proof of safety by itself; it makes the rules and changes independently inspectable.

18. Current status

Fixed economics and the sale foundation have been extensively tested, and public Devnet core protocol integration has passed. The pre-launch allocation software is prepared as a release candidate. Production Mainnet deployment, a production RLYA mint and public token launch are not claimed until their corresponding owner-signed evidence exists.
